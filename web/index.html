About me

I study computer science at George Mason University in Fairfax, Virginia. Before Mason I earned a bachelor's degree in electronics and telecommunications, and that background still shapes how I build things: measure first, assume nothing, and keep the system small enough that you can reason about all of it at once.

Most of my time goes to three things. I teach — I'm a graduate teaching assistant for ECE 334, the linear electronics lab, where I help students debug op-amp circuits that look correct on paper. I write software, mostly computer vision and embedded projects that run on modest hardware. And I work with GMU Dining Services on inventory and shipment tracking, which turned out to be a good lesson in how much of real engineering is just data nobody has organized yet.

Two of my papers have been published, one with Elsevier and one with Springer, both on machine learning at the edge. Outside of coursework I'm usually rebuilding something I already finished, because the second version is always the interesting one.

What I've been building

  • Real-time face detection and tracking

    A YOLOv8 detector trained on WIDER FACE, paired with ByteTrack so identities hold steady across frames instead of flickering between them.

  • Vision-based form coach

    MediaPipe pose estimation that counts repetitions and flags bad form during a workout, running live on a laptop webcam with no cloud round trip.

  • A compression engine, written from scratch

    Arithmetic coding, domain-specific transforms, and Reed–Solomon error correction in pure Python — slow on purpose, so every stage stays readable.
